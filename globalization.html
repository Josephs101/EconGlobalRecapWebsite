Globalization:

 In economics, globalization is the concept that countries are interconnected through their trade, technology, and people. It refers to how the world is now more closely connected, with far more international interactions.

 Globalization has many advantages, as it creates trade opportunities and gives consumers a broader range of products at lower prices. Globalization isn’t limited to the individual scale either, as entire governments can benefit from new technologies and international investments. Globalization has also increased the world’s diversity, with easier access to international music, food, and fashion.

 However, globalization also comes with some disadvantages, given that interconnectivity allows disasters such as pandemics to spread from one country to another faster. There are also environmental concerns that have arisen from the ramp-up in manufacturing and transportation.

Interdependence:

Interdependence means that countries rely on one another for goods and services. This is because no country can produce everything it needs, so countries have to specialize and trade with one another. With many countries relying on imports of food and oil, especially. During the pandemic, many countries experienced disruptions in supply chains as countries closed borders, which is a testament to how interconnected the world has become. While interdependence allows for better cooperation and stronger economies, it also creates risks due to these dependencies.

Globalization increases interdependence as countries naturally depend on each other more as they become more connected.
