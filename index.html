Globalization and Interdependence

In economics, globalization is the concept that countries are interconnected through their trade, technology, and people. It refers to how the world is now more closely connected, with far more international interactions.

Interdependence means that countries rely on one another for goods and services. This is a consequence of the fact that no country can produce everything they need, so countries have to specialize and trade with one another.

Globalization increases interdependence as countries naturally depend on each other more as they become more connected.

Trade Agreements

An important trade agreement would be CUSMA (Canada - US - Mexico Agreement), which is the trade agreement that allows for better trade within North America, replacing the older NAFTA since 2020.


Important Conditions:

  • Regional Value Content Requirements
  • Labour Standards
  • Environmental Protections

Trade Barriers

Tariffs:

A tariff is a tax imposed on imported goods. Governments will leverage tariffs to make foreign products more expensive to encourage people to purchase domestically produced goods instead.


Quotas:

A quota is a limit on the amount of a good that can be imported and exported in a certain period of time.


Embargos:

An embargo is a government mandate that stops trade with another country completely, usually due to political reasons.
