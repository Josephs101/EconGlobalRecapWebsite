Tariffs:

 A tariff is a tax imposed on imported goods. Governments will leverage tariffs to make foreign products more expensive to encourage people to purchase domestically produced goods instead. In addition, governments can also use tariffs to generate revenue or as a response to unfair trade practices. However, tariffs will increase prices for the consumer and can also lead to disputes between countries.

Quotas:

 A Quota is a limit on the amount of a good that can be imported and exported in a certain period of time, imposed by the government. Unlike tariffs, quotas restrict the exact quantity of products that can enter a country. They use quotas to protect domestic businesses from competition, which can stabilize prices at home. For example, countries may limit food imports to support their local farmers. Thus, it can reduce competition but lead to fewer choices and higher costs.

Embargo:

 An Embargo is a government mandate that stops trade with another country completely, usually due to political reasons. Governments choose whether to block all trades or only specific industries. They use them as a way to pressure another country to change policies without having to directly interfere with the military. They can significantly affect economies by blocking the trade of important goods.
